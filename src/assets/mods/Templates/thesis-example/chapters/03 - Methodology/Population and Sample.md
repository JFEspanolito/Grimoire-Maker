# Population and Sample

This section defines the universe of observation, the criteria used to select subjects or data points, and the statistical or theoretical sampling procedure.

## Population Definition

- **Target Population**: The complete theoretical aggregate of elements (individuals, organizations, code commits, transactions) that share common, observable characteristics.
- **Accessible Population**: The subset of the target population realistically accessible within the spatial and temporal scope of this study.

## Eligibility Criteria

- **Inclusion Criteria**: Explicit attributes required for an element to qualify for selection (e.g., *active enterprise repositories with >500 stars and continuous commits over the past 24 months*).
- **Exclusion Criteria**: Specific conditions that disqualify otherwise eligible elements to prevent confounding variables (e.g., *abandoned forks, incomplete survey responses, unverified accounts*).

## Sampling Strategy

- **Sampling Technique**:
  - *Probabilistic*: Simple random, stratified, systematic, or cluster sampling.
  - *Non-Probabilistic*: Purposive, convenience, quota, or snowball sampling.
- **Sample Size Calculation**: State the statistical formula applied (e.g., Cochran's formula, Yamane, or $G*\text{Power}$ power analysis) along with specific parameters: confidence level ($1 - \alpha$, typically 95%), margin of error ($e$, typically 5%), and expected proportion ($p/q$).
- **Sample Representativeness**: Discuss the response rate and assess potential non-response bias.
