# References

The references section provides a comprehensive, rigorous bibliographic record of all scholarly works, datasets, standards, and legal statutes cited in the dissertation.

## Scholarly Citation Guidelines

- **Consistent Citation Style**: Maintain absolute formatting fidelity to the target citation manual prescribed by your university or department (e.g., APA 7th Edition, IEEE, Chicago Author-Date, Harvard).
- **Primary Source Integrity**: Verify that every in-text citation ($Author, Year$) corresponds to an entry in the References list, and that no unreferenced sources appear in this list.
- **Persistent Digital Identifiers**: Include active DOIs (`https://doi.org/...`) or canonical URLs for all journal articles, conference papers, and electronic datasets.
- **Reference Management Integration**: Format references cleanly to allow seamless round-trip synchronization with reference management tools such as Zotero, Mendeley, or BibTeX.

### Example Formatting (APA 7th Edition)

```text
Berners-Lee, T., Fielding, R., & Masinter, L. (2005). Uniform Resource Identifier (URI): Generic Syntax (RFC 3986). Internet Engineering Task Force. https://doi.org/10.17487/RFC3986

Dean, J., & Ghemawat, S. (2008). MapReduce: Simplified data processing on large clusters. Communications of the ACM, 51(1), 107-113. https://doi.org/10.1145/1327452.1327492

Lamport, L. (1998). The part-time parliament. ACM Transactions on Computer Systems, 16(2), 133-169. https://doi.org/10.1145/279227.279229
```
