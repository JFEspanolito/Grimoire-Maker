# Objectives and Justification

This section defines what the study aims to achieve and explains why conducting this research is academically necessary, practically beneficial, and methodologically sound.

## Research Objectives

Objectives must be formulated using measurable action verbs (e.g., Bloom's Taxonomy: *Determine*, *Evaluate*, *Design*, *Correlate*, *Analyze*):

### General Objective
- State the comprehensive outcome of the investigation in a single sentence. It must directly mirror the central research question.

### Specific Objectives
- List 3 to 4 sequential, operational milestones required to achieve the general objective:
  1. *Diagnose / Assess*: Evaluate the baseline state or identify theoretical constructs.
  2. *Design / Develop*: Formulate the model, algorithm, intervention, or experimental protocol.
  3. *Validate / Measure*: Execute the empirical testing and calculate observed metrics.
  4. *Synthesize / Recommend*: Compare outcomes and formulate structured conclusions.

## Justification of the Study

Demonstrate why this research warrants scholarly and economic resources:

- **Theoretical Justification**: How does this fill gaps in current literature? Does it challenge existing models or extend a theory into an unstudied domain?
- **Practical Justification**: Who benefits from this study? How does it resolve operational bottlenecks, reduce costs, or improve end-user outcomes?
- **Methodological Justification**: Does this study develop a novel instrument, refine an experimental protocol, or establish a reproducible benchmark?

## Scope and Delimitations

- Explicitly define the spatial boundaries (institutions, regions), temporal range (duration of observation), and theoretical limits (variables deliberately excluded from analysis).
