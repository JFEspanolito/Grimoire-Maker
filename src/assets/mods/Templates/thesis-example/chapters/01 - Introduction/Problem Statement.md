# Problem Statement

The introduction establishes the academic foundation and urgency of the investigation, contextualizing the specific phenomenon under study within broader industry or scholarly realities.

## Structuring the Problem

Organize the problem statement using an inverted-pyramid approach (macro to micro):

1. **Macro Context (Global/Domain Background)**: Provide factual, referenced evidence of how this issue manifests globally across the discipline. What trends, technological shifts, or societal challenges define the current landscape?
2. **Meso Context (Institutional/Local Reality)**: Describe the specific environment, industry segment, population, or geographical boundary where the study takes place.
3. **Micro Context (The Concrete Gap)**: Articulate the precise pain point, unanswered theoretical dilemma, or operational breakdown. Distinguish symptoms (what is visible, such as latency spikes or high turnover) from root causes (underlying structural flaws).
4. **Central Research Question**: Formulate the primary interrogative sentence that drives the entire investigation. It must be researchable, non-trivial, and clear.
5. **Secondary Research Sub-Questions**: Deconstruct the central question into sequential sub-questions addressing diagnosis, methodological analysis, and solution evaluation.

## Key Checkpoints

- Ground every empirical claim in recent scholarly sources or verified industry metrics.
- Avoid proposing solutions prematurely in this chapter; the focus must strictly remain on characterizing the problem.
