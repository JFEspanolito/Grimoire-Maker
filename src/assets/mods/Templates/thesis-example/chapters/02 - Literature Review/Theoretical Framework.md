# Theoretical Framework

The theoretical framework anchors the thesis within established academic theory, defining the concepts, constructs, and analytical models used to interpret empirical findings.

## Guidelines for this Section

- **Core Paradigm and Theories**: Identify the seminal theories (e.g., Relational Data Models, Diffusion of Innovations, Cognitive Load Theory) that underpin your conceptual approach. Cite primary sources where the theories were originally formulated.
- **Conceptual Definitions**: Rigorously define all core terms and independent/dependent variables. Avoid colloquial definitions or dictionary entries; rely exclusively on peer-reviewed literature.
- **Theoretical Models**: Present the conceptual diagram or systemic flow illustrating how variables interact, mediate, or moderate outcomes.
- **Synthesis Over Listing**: Avoid a mere sequential catalog of authors (`Author A says X; Author B says Y`). Group concepts thematically and contrast how different schools of thought define the phenomena under study.
